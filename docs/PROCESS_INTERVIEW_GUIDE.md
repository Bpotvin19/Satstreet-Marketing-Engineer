# Process Interview Guide

Use this in 20–30 minute conversations with each function owner.

## Opening

> I'm mapping how work actually gets done so we can identify where AI or automation could remove repetitive work, improve client service, or help create more revenue. I'm not trying to automate people's judgment — I want to find the work around the judgment that machines can handle better.

## Core questions

1. What do you do every day or week that is repetitive?
2. What task do you procrastinate because it is tedious?
3. What information do people constantly ask you for?
4. What do you copy and paste between systems?
5. What reports do you manually assemble?
6. What requires you to search old emails, Notion, Slack/Telegram, CRM notes, or files?
7. Where do mistakes or delays happen most often?
8. What depends on you personally remembering to do something?
9. What work interrupts your highest-value responsibility?
10. What takes 30 minutes today that should take 30 seconds?
11. What work would you **not** trust AI to do?
12. What would you gladly delegate to an assistant if you had one?
13. If we could fix only one workflow for you this month, what would it be?

## Process capture

For every opportunity uncovered, record:

- Process name
- Owner
- Trigger
- Frequency
- Current steps
- Systems touched
- Input data
- Output
- Minutes per run
- Approximate runs per month
- Biggest pain point
- Client / revenue impact
- Risk if wrong
- Human approval point
- Suggested future workflow

## Department-specific prompts

### Sales
- How do you decide who to call today?
- How much time goes into prospect research?
- How are stale leads surfaced?
- How do you know when an existing client may be worth calling?
- What happens after a meeting?

### Trading
- What information do you repeatedly need from sales?
- Which client updates are manual?
- What reporting / handoff work can be standardized?

### Compliance
- Which administrative tasks consume time around actual compliance judgment?
- What questions are repeatedly answered from approved policies?
- Which processes require mandatory human control?

### Client Service
- What questions are answered repeatedly?
- What should a client be able to self-serve safely?
- What must always reach a human?

### Marketing
- Where do ideas come from?
- What slows publication?
- Which content generates actual business conversations?
- What facts are repeatedly checked?

### Management
- What information do you wish you had every Monday morning?
- What gets manually assembled for meetings?
- Where do you lack visibility into execution or ROI?

## End-state question

> If this worked perfectly six months from now, what would the team be doing more of — and what would they almost never have to do manually again?
