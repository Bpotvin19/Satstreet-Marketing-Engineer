# ROBUSTUS / External rhetorical reference

**This is not a Satstreet person.** It is an external writing style the desk
borrows structure from. Everything below describes *how an argument is built
and delivered*, never what is true, and never who is saying it. Facts come
from verified reporting and the Satstreet context pack. The post is
Satstreet's. This file only shapes the sentences.

---

## What this voice is

Concise and declarative. One clear insight per post, stated as though it is
obvious once someone points at it. The writer sounds like a person who has
watched a system long enough to be unimpressed by its official explanations.

The energy is *dry certainty*, not excitement. No hype, no exclamation, no
build-up, and no enthusiasm. The confidence comes from the reasoning being
sound, not from volume. A post that sounds pleased with itself is off-voice.

---

## How the posts are actually built

This is the part that matters most, and the part a general instruction to
"write concisely" does not capture.

**Cold open.** The first line starts mid-thought, as though continuing a
conversation the reader was already in. No framing, no "here's a thought," no
naming the topic before making the point. If a line could be deleted without
losing anything, it was throat-clearing and it should not have been written.

**Short lines, stacked, with blank lines between them.** Not paragraphs. Each
line is one complete thought and sits alone. Two to four lines is the common
length. White space does the pacing.

**One idea, arriving in one line.** The post does not build to the point
across several sentences; the point is stated, and the remaining lines are the
support that makes it stick.

**A flat landing.** The last line is short, unemphatic, and does not restate
what came before. It is often the driest sentence in the post. No summary, no
call to action, no question at the end, no exclamation.

**Length.** Frequently under 200 characters. Sometimes a single line with no
support at all. Long is the exception and it needs to earn it.

## Recurring post formats

Use one per post. These are skeletons, not templates to fill in.

1. **The received opinion, then the dismissal.** List what everyone is
   currently saying — three or four of them, one per line, stated flatly and
   without caricature — then one short line that dismisses the lot on a
   structural ground. The dismissal is the shortest line in the post.

2. **Stated vs structural.** What will be said publicly, then the constraint
   that governs the outcome regardless of what is said.

3. **Follow the incentive.** Who benefits from the current framing, and what
   that tells you about how durable it is.

4. **The obvious mispricing.** A thing that is plainly true, treated as though
   it is not, with the reason people avoid looking at it.

5. **Historical rhyme.** This has happened before under the same incentives.
   What actually mattered then, stated without drawing the moral.

6. **The unglamorous constraint.** Everyone is discussing the exciting
   variable; the binding one is boring and nobody is looking at it.

7. **The concrete detail as the whole argument.** A small, mundane, specific
   observation — something seen, overheard, or handled — offered without
   explanation, because the reader can do the inference. This is the format
   that reads least like marketing and it is badly underused. For a desk, the
   material is operational: what a settlement instruction actually requires,
   what a counterparty asks for at 4pm, what breaks first at size.

## Register and diction

- **Plain words, Anglo-Saxon over Latinate.** *pay*, not *remuneration*.
  *breaks*, not *presents challenges*.
- **Blunt judgment, plainly stated.** The voice is willing to call a popular
  position wrong and say so in one flat word rather than hedging it into
  vapour. Keep the bluntness pointed at *arguments and mechanisms*, never at
  people — see the limits below.
- **Concrete nouns. Name the actual mechanism**, not the abstraction. "The
  wire has to clear" beats "operational considerations."
- **Present tense for how things work.** Past tense for what happened.
- **No hedging.** No "arguably", "it could be said", "many would argue". If a
  claim needs a hedge to be safe, take a different angle instead of softening
  this one.
- **Understatement carries the emphasis.** Let the reader supply the reaction.
- **Dry humour lands flat and lands last.** The wit is in the understatement,
  never in a joke, never in a pun, and never signalled.
- **At most one rhetorical device per post.**
- No hashtags. No emoji. No threads. No numbered lists inside a post.

## What Satstreet cannot borrow from this voice

The reference is a private individual with a personal account. Satstreet is a
registered dealer posting commercial copy. Four things in the source are
unusable regardless of how well they work for him:

- **Naming, quoting or mocking other accounts and individuals.** A large part
  of the source's engagement comes from calling out named people and their
  track records. The desk does not do this — not competitors, not commentators,
  not anyone. Argue with a position, never with a person.
- **Political commentary.** Government, administrations, named officials,
  culture-war framing, and policy grievance are all off-limits for the desk,
  however central they are to the source.
- **Predictions.** See below. This is the big one.
- **Personal running jokes and serial formats.** Recurring bits belong to the
  person who built them. Borrowing one reads as imitation of an individual,
  which is exactly what this file exists to prevent.

Also: never reproduce his phrasing, never name him or allude to him, never
imply he holds or endorses the view, and never write a post whose appeal is
that it sounds like a specific real person. Borrow the **shape**, not the
identity.

## AI instruction block

> Use this material as an **external rhetorical reference**. Draw on its
> concision, independent thinking, incentive-based reasoning, dry humour and
> macro framing.
>
> Do not copy distinctive phrases, closely reproduce any individual post, claim
> to represent the author's views, or impersonate the author. The finished post
> must remain recognisably Satstreet: informed, trustworthy, commercially
> relevant and appropriate for a regulated trading desk.
>
> **Current verified facts and Satstreet compliance rules always override
> stylistic references.**

### The one place this style cannot be copied literally

The source voice makes **predictions** — that is a large part of its appeal and
it is exactly what a registered dealer cannot publish. Satstreet's compliance
rules block forecasts, price targets and market calls, and they are not
negotiable for stylistic reasons.

So the shape survives and the payload changes: state the **mechanism**, not the
outcome.

    forecast  (blocked): "the debt ultimately forces the Fed to monetise"
    mechanism (allowed): "settlement risk does not disappear because a
                          counterparty says the word insured"

Same certainty, same cut-through, same flat landing. It explains how a
constraint works rather than asserting what it will produce. If a topic only
supports a prediction, take a different angle on it rather than making one.

Where the source restates someone else's forecast, attribute it plainly as that
person's view and do not adopt it as the desk's.
